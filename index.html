AI has crossed thresholds that sounded like fiction a decade ago. Companies have not. Every quarter, the distance grows between what a company could do and what it gets done. The constraint is not the machine. It is the company around it — still built for a world where thinking was expensive.

Inside companies, AI assists. Answers, drafts, suggestions — brilliant bursts that end when attention moves on. A person still carries the goal, feeds the context, cleans up the misses. But a business does not run on bursts. It runs on states held steady: the pipeline full, the promise kept, the cost in line. Assistance cannot hold a state. A loop can.

Engineers solved this once. The steam engine alone did not make the Industrial Revolution. Early engines surged and stalled with the load; a worker stood at the valve all day, correcting by hand. Then Watt fitted the governor — two spinning weights that sense the speed and work the valve themselves. Set the speed once, and the machine holds it. Nobody chained to a valve. The power was already there. What made it industry was the loop.

Telic builds that governor for the modern company. You declare the state a part of your business must reach and hold — renewals above ninety-five percent, every ticket answered inside the hour, margin intact — and draw the limits it must respect. The machinery does the rest: measure, act, verify, undo what fails. Doing this for real means running for months without drifting, reading the business as it is rather than as it reports itself, and learning the rules that exist only in the heads of the people who run things.

None of this removes people. It promotes them. The ones closest to the work set the destination and keep command — approving what is consequential, overriding what is wrong, reading logs that hide nothing. When the machinery is unsure, it does not guess. It asks. A copilot waits for your hands; a governor keeps the speed.

The companies that matter in ten years will be the ones that rebuild around held outcomes first. Not ten percent faster — a different kind of company: declared states coming true on schedule, judgment spent where only people can spend it, attention supplied by machinery that never runs out.

We take on a few outcomes at a time, with the people who own them, and we price the way conviction prices: paid on outcomes, not effort.
